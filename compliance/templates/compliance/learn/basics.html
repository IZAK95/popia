The Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA) is South Africa's privacy law. It has been fully enforceable since 1 July 2021. It sets rules for how organisations collect, use, store, share and destroy information about people.

Does it apply to my business?

Almost certainly yes. POPIA applies to anyone in South Africa who processes personal information in a record – on a computer or on paper. There is no exemption for small businesses. If you have employees, customers or suppliers, you process personal information.

Two things surprise most people:

  • Companies are protected too. Unlike most privacy laws abroad, POPIA protects information about existing juristic persons (companies, trusts) as well as individuals. Your B2B customer list is covered.
  • "Processing" means almost anything: collecting, receiving, recording, storing, updating, using, sharing, emailing, archiving, even deleting.

What do I actually have to do?

In practical terms, a small business needs to:

  1. Have an Information Officer (usually you) and register them with the Information Regulator.
  2. Know what personal information you have, why, where it is and who you share it with.
  3. Only use it for legitimate purposes, keep it only as long as needed, and keep it secure.
  4. Tell people what you do with their information (privacy notices).
  5. Have a PAIA manual and submit a yearly PAIA report.
  6. Handle requests from people and report breaches.
  7. Have contracts with service providers who handle the information for you.
  8. Train your staff and review everything yearly.

This app turns each of these into steps in the checklist.

What if I ignore it?

The Information Regulator can issue enforcement notices and administrative fines of up to R10 million; serious offences carry up to 10 years' imprisonment. People can also sue for damages. More realistically for a small business: a breach you didn't prepare for, a customer complaint you can't answer, or a large client's supplier questionnaire you can't pass.
