POPIA calls a data breach a security compromise. Section 22 applies when there are reasonable grounds to believe that personal information has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person.

Important difference from Europe: POPIA has no "unlikely to result in harm" exemption. If the test above is met, you must notify both the Regulator and the affected people – even for a single misdirected email.

Who, when and how

  • When: as soon as reasonably possible after discovery, taking into account law-enforcement needs and the steps needed to establish the scope and restore integrity. There is no fixed number of hours, but think days, not weeks.
  • The Regulator: via form SCN1 on the eServices portal – mandatory since 1 April 2025 (email submissions are no longer accepted).
  • The affected people: in writing – email, letter, prominent website notice or news media – with enough information to protect themselves: possible consequences, what you've done, what they should do, and the identity of the unauthorised person if known.
  • Operators must tell you immediately if they have a breach involving your information (s21(2)).

Examples

SituationNotifiable?
Unencrypted laptop with HR files stolen from a carYes
Encrypted, password-protected laptop lost; remote-wipedUsually no – record the reasoning
Payslip emailed to the wrong employee who opened itYes
Email account hacked via phishing; mailbox contains customer dataYes (the attacker had access)
Ransomware encrypted files; attackers claim to have copied themYes
Staff member viewed colleague's medical file without needYes

Numbers

The Regulator received 2,374 security compromise notifications in 2024/25 and a 40% year-on-year increase in early 2025/26, and is building a dedicated team to scrutinise breach handling. Preparing now – encryption, MFA and a plan – is far cheaper than responding later.
