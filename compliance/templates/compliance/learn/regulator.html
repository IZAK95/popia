The Information Regulator is an independent body that monitors and enforces POPIA and PAIA.

  • Website: inforegulator.org.za
  • eServices portal: eservices.inforegulator.org.za – IO registration, breach reports, PAIA reports, complaints
  • Address: Woodmead North Office Park, 54 Maxwell Drive, Woodmead, Johannesburg, 2191
  • General enquiries: enquiries@inforegulator.org.za

What can it do?

  • Investigate complaints and act on its own initiative, including assessments and inspections.
  • Issue enforcement notices requiring you to take or stop specific steps.
  • Issue infringement notices with administrative fines of up to R10 million (s109).
  • Refer criminal offences – e.g. obstructing the Regulator or failing to comply with an enforcement notice – which carry fines or up to 10 years' imprisonment (s107).

Separately, people can claim damages from you in civil court (s99). They don't have to prove you acted intentionally or negligently; your defences are limited, for example that compliance wasn't reasonably practicable in the circumstances.

If the Regulator contacts you

  1. Log it in Regulator correspondence the same day. The app tracks the deadline and shows what to do for each kind of notice.
  2. Information and enforcement notices can be appealed to the High Court within 30 days of receiving them (s97). Get legal advice early.
  3. Answer truthfully: a false statement in response to an information notice is an offence (s103(2)).
  4. An infringement notice (fine) must be paid, arranged in instalments, or taken to court within 30 days (s109).

Enforcement so far

The largest fines to date (R5 million each) went to government departments: the Department of Justice (after a 2021 ransomware attack and failing to comply with an enforcement notice) and the Department of Basic Education (publishing matric results – later set aside on appeal). The Regulator has also issued enforcement notices to private companies, including for security compromises and PAIA non-compliance, and its 2025/26 plan signals tougher scrutiny of breach handling.

How the Regulator usually finds you

  • A complaint from an employee, customer or member of the public.
  • A breach you (or a service provider) report.
  • Compliance sweeps – e.g. checking for missing PAIA reports or unregistered Information Officers.
