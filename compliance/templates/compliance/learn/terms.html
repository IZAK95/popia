Personal information
Any information about an identifiable, living person or an existing company: name, ID number, contact details, address, bank details, salary, opinions, correspondence, photos, online identifiers, and more.
Special personal information
Extra-sensitive categories: religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, health, sex life, biometrics (fingerprints, face scans) and criminal behaviour. Prohibited unless a specific exception applies (s26–s33).
Data subject
The person (or company) the information is about – your employees, customers, supplier contacts, job applicants.
Responsible party
The organisation that decides why and how personal information is processed – that's your business.
Operator
An outside party processing information for you under your instructions – e.g. payroll software provider, cloud email, IT support, your bookkeeper. You need a written contract with each (s21).
Processing
Anything you do with personal information, from collecting to deleting.
Information Officer (IO)
The person responsible for POPIA compliance – by default the head of the business. Must be registered with the Regulator.
Information Regulator
The independent body that enforces POPIA and PAIA. Runs the eServices portal for registrations, breach reports and PAIA reports.
Consent
A voluntary, specific and informed expression of will. Only one of six lawful grounds – and not usually the best one, because it can be withdrawn.
Security compromise
POPIA's term for a data breach: personal information accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person.
Direct marketing
Approaching a person to promote or sell goods or services, or to ask for donations.
PAIA
The Promotion of Access to Information Act – the companion law giving people the right to request records from organisations.
De-identify
Removing anything that could identify the person, so the information can no longer be linked back to them.
